# LinkedIn post: Media Literacy Week

Last week was Media Literacy Week. It is a useful moment to think about what media literacy means in the age of generative AI.

Media literacy is no longer only about spotting fake news. It also means checking a fluent AI answer against the source it came from:

- Where does this claim come from?
- Which passage supports it?
- What does the source actually say – and what did the AI add?
- Which perspectives or contradictions are missing?

Software should not hide that step. It should make it easier.

AI Summary Helper summarizes webpages and research papers and takes every answer back to the passage it is based on. Learners, educators and institutions can get oriented faster without losing sight of the original source.

That matters in education: AI should not only produce finished answers. It should encourage close reading, source checking and lasting understanding.

The education pitch shows the workflow in ten steps – from understanding and verification to connecting topics and sources in a knowledge graph:

https://ai-summary-helper.byphil.eu/education/

Open source, no mandatory account and a choice of model – including local use with Ollama.
