How to

Send an article to your Kindle in three steps

Step 1

Approve the sender once

In your Amazon account, add kindle@byphil.eu to the approved personal document e-mail list.

Step 2

Save the article

Open it in Chrome or Firefox and let AI Summary Helper archive it, with its images compressed.

Step 3

Send it

Choose Send to Kindle, pick EPUB or EPUB with images, and the article shows up on your Kindle.

Questions

Archive and e-reader questions

How do I send a web article to my Kindle from Chrome or Firefox?

Install AI Summary Helper, add kindle@byphil.eu to the approved personal document e-mail list in your Amazon account once, then archive an article and choose Send to Kindle. Articles arrive as EPUB, with or without images.

Does it cost anything?

The extension is free and open source. The first three Kindle sends are free; unlimited Kindle sends need the Support Pass. Sending with LocalSend is free and never touches our server.

Can I use it with a Kobo or another e-reader?

Yes, on devices that run KOReader: install KOReader’s LocalSend plugin, start the receiver and send from the extension over your Wi-Fi. Readers without KOReader, such as Tolino and PocketBook, are not supported directly yet.

What is LocalSend?

LocalSend is an open-source app that sends files directly between devices on the same network. The file goes from your browser to the device, with no cloud in between.

Are images kept when I archive an article?

Yes. Images are resized to a maximum width and compressed, so the article stays readable and small.

Does my archive leave my device?

The archive stays in your browser. Sending to Kindle is the exception: Amazon only accepts documents by e-mail from approved senders, so the article goes through our service for that send. LocalSend and AirDrop go directly to your device.
