What it does

Everything a reading list should have done from the start

Save it, understand it, find it again, and actually get back to it — without another subscription holding your archive hostage.

Everything a reading list should have done from the start.

Everything a reading list should have done from the start. You choose the model, the server, and whether anything leaves your machine at all. The problem was never saving. It's getting back to what you saved — this is built around that part. Every save becomes searchable, connected, and exportable — a real archive, not a growing pile. Follow what you care about, see the good news first, and get the day summed up. Read, listen or send it to your Kindle — whenever you have a free minute.

A built-in RSS reader

Follow blogs and news sites, filter by source, tag, date or mood, import your OPML, and open or summarize any item in one click. Play podcast feeds right in the extension, and get an optional AI recap of each day from the headlines. Favorites land in your archive, and feeds are stored on your device.

YouTube videos, from their captions

Open a video and summarize it. The extension reads the captions, writes the key points in your language, and follow-up answers cite the passage they come from.

PDFs, like any other page

Open a PDF in the browser, or attach one from disk, and summarize it like any page. “What I used” shows what the model read: words, tokens and model.

20+ languages, cross-platform

Read and summarize in your language of choice. Extension, native side panel, or bookmarklet — for wherever the browser extension can't reach.

AI summaries, your choice of model

OpenAI, Gemini, Mistral, DeepSeek, or a custom endpoint — or point it at a local Ollama model and never touch the network for this step at all.

Ghost highlights, right on the page

The AI lightly highlights the most important verbatim passages in the article — so you can skim the key insights at a glance, and they travel with the summary to Kindle, LocalSend, or Markdown.

Research mode: papers recognised by their DOI

Scientific papers are recognised from their DOI and citation metadata and flagged as trial, review or preprint. Design, sample and limitations are pulled out, and the citation is built from the DOI registry, not by the AI.

Compare sources on one question

Bundle saved articles in a collection, type each source (peer-reviewed, preprint, news, opinion), run the lateral-reading check, and let the AI compare the summaries: where they agree, disagree and what is missing.

Instant read: listen while it writes

The summary is spoken while it is still being written: the title first, then sentence by sentence, with pause, mute and speed. Read it again from any card.

Real search, on-device

Full-text search across titles, tags, and article content — powered by a TF-IDF index built in your browser. No round-trip, no lag, nothing sent anywhere.

A knowledge graph, not just a list

See how your saved articles connect — by shared tags, and by topic similarity even when they don't share a tag at all.

Save for later, on purpose

Mark a "decision" with a reason and a timeframe, and get reminded — instead of watching it disappear into a pile you'll never open again.

A digest that picks itself

One tap curates a small, varied set of your saves — not five versions of the same story — bundled into one file for Kindle or LocalSend.

Mood filter and mood over time

The AI rates headlines from heavy to good. Show only the good news with one tap, and follow the mood of your reading by day, week and month.

Your library inside Claude, LM Studio and Cursor

Switch on AI apps in Settings, and Claude Desktop, LM Studio, Cursor and other MCP apps on your computer can search your library, quote your highlights and list what is due. If you allow it, they can also save links for you. Nothing goes through our server, and you choose what they may read.

Your library on your own storage, encrypted

Sync and back up your library to your own Nextcloud, WebDAV server, S3 bucket or a folder on your computer. Everything is encrypted in your browser first, so the server only sees scrambled files. You can also publish a list to your own website, with search, a tag graph and a 3D map. Off until you add a place; we run no sync server and see nothing. How to set it up · Why it matters

Notes and comments on any page

Select text and choose: highlight it, comment on it, or save a note with the quote. Everything lands in a Notes tab, as a list or a grid of sticky notes, by article or by tag, and syncs with the rest of your library.

Export to Obsidian, Markdown, Word and PDF

Send a summary straight to your Obsidian vault, or export it as Markdown, Word (DOCX), OpenDocument or PDF, with the title, source, tags and your highlights in one note.

An analytics report of your reading

Reading streak, time saved by summaries, activity, topics over the weeks and a word cloud, computed from your own library.

Questions

Before you install

Do I need an account to use it?

No. Install it, bring your own AI provider key (or use a local Ollama model), and start saving. An account only matters if you want byphil Cloud (many AI models without a key) or more Kindle/LocalSend sends, which a donation unlocks.

What actually leaves my browser?

Only the summarization request — sent to whichever AI provider you configured — plus, if you use the RSS reader, requests to the feeds you subscribed to, and, only when you click an AI recap, the headlines and short snippets of that day. Search, duplicate detection, tag cleanup, reading-level and sentiment scoring, and "similar articles" recommendations all run locally against your own saved archive.

Can I run this with zero cloud dependency?

Yes — point it at a local Ollama model and the summarization step stays on your machine too. Everything else already does.

What happens to my saves if I stop supporting the project?

Your archive stays exactly where it's always been — in your browser's local storage on your device. The Support Pass only gates cloud-dependent extras (byphil Cloud AI access, unlimited Kindle/LocalSend sends), never access to what you've already saved.

How do I install the Android companion?

Download the APK from the link above (or from the latest release), open it, and allow installs from your browser when Android asks. Then pair it by scanning the QR code in the extension under Settings → Phone inbox. It is not on Google Play yet.

Is this open source?

Yes — the extension's source is public on GitHub, under the AGPL-3.0 license. It is free for personal, educational and non-profit use; companies that want to use it outside the AGPL's terms can get a commercial license.
